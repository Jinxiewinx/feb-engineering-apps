## 1. Purpose

Cutting and sanding cured CF is where we make our dust (health problem), our edges (quality problem), and, in SN5, some of our damage: we punctured catch cans sanding thin laminate (2026-05-27), and Dremel work on molds ate heads and marred surfaces. These are the rules for cutting, sanding, and coating cured parts.

## 2. Scope

Cured CF, glass and G10 parts and panels: trimming to scribe lines, edge finishing, surface prep for bonding, bonding inserts and hardware into CF tube and parts (§7.6), and clear-coating. Flat-panel cutting by waterjet, ShopSabre or bandsaw is in scope (§7.5).

Drilling holes and cutting openings [lead input needed]. The SN5 seat cracked above a cutout and a side bolt pulled through it (#composites, 2026-09-28, ts 1790610889.634989). This standard sets no hole sizes, edge distances or washer rules until a lead or a test supplies them.

Not covered: temporary sprays and powders used for 3D scanning, until the metrology study concludes (#composites, 2026-08-29; ts 1790837965.882759).

## 4. Definitions

- **Sacrificial ply**: the 88 spread-tow outer layer specified in the stack (CS-002 §7.1 step 3) to absorb sanding. It is the sanding budget; structural plies are not.
- **Trim line**: the part's cut boundary, transferred from the mold's scribe lines at demold.
- **Keying**: abrading a bond face so adhesive has something to grip. The team uses 120 grit; the DP420 TDS asks only for clean, fine-grit abrasive (R1).
- **Open time**: the longest a joint may wait after adhesive goes on a face before it is closed and fixed. 20 min for DP420 (R1).
- **Handling strength**: the point where a bonded joint can be unclamped and moved, not loaded. 2.5 h at 23 °C (73 °F) for DP420 (R1).
- **Load-carrying bond**: any bond that carries load on the car: an insert, a rod end, a mount, a bracket.

- **Supervision rule (set in SN5; the Composites Lead may change it): no cutting cured carbon without a second trained person present.** Trained means the person holds the Trimming and finishing training in the app. The Composites Lead adds that training in People → Catalog ("Add a training", CS standard CS-009) and may gate the "Trim and finish" step on it from Reports → Layup techniques → Edit steps ("Training to sign").
- Waterjet per Jacobs shop rules.
- **Clear coat (LiME LiNE UC4:1):** the Part A SDS (R6) classes it as a highly flammable liquid and vapor (H225) that may cause an allergic skin reaction (H317), causes serious eye irritation (H319) and may cause drowsiness or dizziness (H336). It says to use it only outdoors or in a well-ventilated area and to wear protective gloves, protective clothing, and eye and face protection. Part B, the activator, is an isocyanate (`03 Datasheets/INDEX.md`, LiME LiNE row) and its SDS is not on file. Nobody shall open, mix or apply Part B until its SDS, or the hazard text from the can's label, is in `03 Datasheets/` and this section quotes its PPE.

## 7. Procedure

### 7.1 Trim

1. Trim no earlier than the FEB hold for the resin system (CS-008 §5). On the run, "Trim and finish" comes after "Cure and demould" ("Cure" on a Foam wrapped run), the step that carries that hold.
2. Mark the cut from the scribe-transferred trim line. Tape the cut path: the tape controls chips and shows the line under dust.
3. Cut with the extractor running and a second trained person present (§6). Cut outside the line and sand to it; a Dremel does not steer well enough to cut on it.

### 7.2 Sand

1. Read the run's stack before sanding anything. The sanding budget is the sacrificial ply and only the sacrificial ply (Figure 1).
2. Zones under 3 plies: hand-sand only, count strokes, check often. Machine-sanding thin laminate is how the catch cans died (R2).

![Left: the sacrificial ply is the sanding budget; structural plies are not an allowance. Right: the catch-can failure, a machine sander on thin laminate at a taper, through the skin and into the substrate.](figures/cs009-sacrificial.v2.png)

3. Edges: 120 → 400 grit. Seal exposed edge fibers with a thin resin wipe where cosmetics or sealing matter; a raw CF edge wicks moisture and splinters into hands.

If structural weave shows past the plan:

4. Stop sanding.
5. Press ⚑ (Report an issue on this step) on "Trim and finish" ("Finish" on a Glass infusion run).
6. Add the spot under "Photos of the defect".
7. Leave the Disposition to the part's engineer or the Composites Lead. The run cannot complete until the issue is disposed.

### 7.3 Bond prep (DP420)

1. Wipe both bond faces with IPA to lift oil and release agent.
2. Abrade both faces with 120 grit until the gloss is gone (team value; the TDS asks for clean, fine-grit abrasive, R1).
3. Wipe both faces again with clean IPA. On cured laminate and metal parts away from any mold, acetone may be used instead, as the TDS's own FRP test prep does (Acetone/Abrade/Acetone, R1). Never acetone on or near tooling board (CS-004 §7.1). Put out ignition sources while solvents are open (R1). Let the faces flash off.
4. Put gloves on and keep them on. Do not touch the bond faces from here.
5. Load the duo-pak cartridge in the EPX applicator, remove the cap, and dispense a little until both sides flow evenly. Then fit the mixing nozzle (R1).
6. DP420 mixes 2:1 by volume (B:A), 2:0.98 by weight; the cartridge sets the ratio (R1). Apply, then close and fix the joint within the 20 min open time (R1).
7. Clamp so squeeze-out forms a continuous fillet, and leave the clamps on until handling strength, 2.5 h at 23 °C (73 °F) (R1). That fillet is the §8 acceptance look. The TDS reports bond strength after 7 days at 23 °C; a joint that will carry load follows §7.6.

### 7.4 Clear coat

1. Do not open, mix or apply Part B (the activator) until its SDS or label hazard text is on file and §6 quotes its PPE.
2. Scuff the part, wipe it with IPA, and apply the part label (CS-001 §7.5). The TDS asks for a substrate free of grease, dirt and contaminants (R5).
3. Mix 4 parts UC4:1 clear with 1 part activator (R5).
4. Use the mix within its pot life, 2 h at 77 °F / 25 °C (R5).
5. Apply 2 medium wet coats, 10 to 15 min apart (R5). To recoat after 24 h, scuff first (R5).
6. Leave the coat to cure before handling: the TDS gives dust free at 30 min and sand and buff at 12 to 24 h air dry, both at 77 °F / 25 °C (R5).
7. Record the activator, the number of coats and the time in the "Trim and finish" note ("Finish" on a Glass infusion run).

### 7.5 Cutting flat panels: waterjet, ShopSabre or bandsaw

1. The DXF comes from the requesting subteam through the WO, never a DM (the dashboard lesson, PP-04). Confirm the revision on the WO before cutting; on a Glass infusion run this is the "Cut to DXF, confirm revision" step.
2. Pick the machine by the job and your training: the Jacobs Hall OMAX waterjet (Jacobs waterjet training; Jacobs shop rules govern it), the RFS ShopSabre (CS-005 governs it; ask RFS staff before machining G10), or the RFS composites bandsaw (RFS composites bandsaw training) (R8).
3. On the ShopSabre and the bandsaw, the §6 dust rules apply: extractor running, respirator on, second trained person present.

### 7.6 Bonding inserts and hardware into CF tube and parts

1. Every load-carrying bond shall use DP420 (R7).
2. Never use 5-minute epoxy or superglue (CA) on a load-carrying joint. In September testing an undertray insert turned out to have been bonded with 5-minute epoxy (R7).
3. Prep both faces per §7.3 steps 1 to 4. On a metal insert or rod end, abrade the full bonded length, not a few file strokes.
4. Mask the threads, and dam the tube behind the insert so adhesive cannot reach the threads or run down the tube. DP420 that reached a rod-end thread locked the rod at a set length (R7).
5. Purge the cartridge until both sides flow before fitting the mixing nozzle (§7.3 step 5).
6. Hold the bondline at the gap the drawing calls for. The TDS's test bondline is 0.13 to 0.20 mm (5 to 8 mil) (R1); the team has not set its own gap.
7. Fixture or clamp the joint so it cannot move until handling strength, 2.5 h at 23 °C (73 °F) (R1).
8. Do not load the joint (mount it on the car, torque a rod end, drive) before the team hold: [lead input needed]. The TDS reports bond strength after 7 days at 23 °C (R1). In September an insert was mounted about an hour after bonding (R7). The app does not enforce this hold; the person bonding does.
9. Record in the note on the step where the bond is done ("Trim and finish"; "Finish" on a Glass infusion run; "Execute" on an Other run): adhesive and lot, prep done, time bonded, and time first loaded.
