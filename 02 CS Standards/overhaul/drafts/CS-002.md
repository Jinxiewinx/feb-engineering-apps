## 1. Purpose

In SN5 our stacks lived in the tracker as shorthand ("6X 195 + CORE", "195 88 .25 NOMEX"). That's the *intent*, not what actually got laid. Orientations, core placement, where the copper mesh went, what we substituted when we ran out of cloth: all of it lived in people's heads. That's how the dashboard got remade 3+ times against a spec that existed nowhere (PP-04), how the UT stack was still up in the air three weeks before layup (PP-07), and why nobody could answer "what stack did the seat use last year?" (PP-09). This doc is how we write a stack down before we build, and record what we actually did after.

## 2. Scope

Every laminated part: infusion, wet layup, foam-wrapped, and forged CF (mass/ratio spec instead of ply table). Applies to R&D coupons too, since untraceable test data is noise.

## 4. Definitions

- **Stack / layup schedule**: the full ordered list of plies from mold surface (P1) outward, including cores, meshes, and inserts.
- **Orientation**: warp direction relative to the part's marked 0° datum. For woven cloth use 0/90 or ±45; for spread-tow/UD state the fiber direction.
- **Cloth naming**: `<gsm> <weave>` from the team's stock: 88 spread-tow, 195 twill, 220 twill, 670 twill (Sigmatex); always gsm, never "the thick one". A cloth whose gsm has not been measured yet is written by type and source until it is, for example `UD carbon, ACP, gsm TBD`. Measuring it is a task to assign, not a footnote.
- **Inserts**: a G10 (or other) insert is a ply in the table like a core, with its material and thickness written in the row. A thickness mismatch between an insert and the core around it is a deviation to record (§7.4).
- **ME / RE**: the part's Mold Engineer (ME) and Manufacturing Engineer (RE), the two engineer fields on the part and on each of its runs. Both are picked from the app's roster, not typed (CS-013 §4).
- **Plan and as-built**: the plan is the stack on the part (Parts tab, Layup stack). The as-built is the copy each run takes when it is started, kept in the run's Stack & BOM section. Editing the run's copy never changes the plan.
- **Stack freeze**: the buy-off of the Stack frozen step on a run (§7.2). From then on, edits to the part's plan no longer reach that run, and the run's stack changes only as §7.2 steps 3 to 6 say.

## 5. Equipment & Materials

None; this is a specification standard. The planned stack lives on the part (Parts tab, Layup stack section, R5). Each run (work order) gets a copy when it is started with + New run, and that copy becomes the run's as-built record in its Stack & BOM section (CS-013 §7.1, §7.2).

## 6. Safety

No physical hazards; specification standard. Shop-floor safety lives in the process standards it feeds (CS-006, CS-007).

## 7. Procedure

### 7.1 Specify (before any manufacturing)

1. The part's Manufacturing Engineer (RE) drafts the stack on the part: Parts tab, Layup stack, + ply, one row per ply from the mold surface outward, in the format of §7.3.
2. Justify the choice in one or two lines per decision: coupon data (R4), the SN5 as-built for a similar part (its WO), or an explicit assumption flagged for testing. New unproven stacks on schedule-critical parts require a coupon or test panel first.
3. Include function plies explicitly: sacrificial 88 spread-tow outer layers for sanding protection, copper mesh position and overlap for grounded parts (CS-010), core chamfers/grooves.
4. State the target: cured thickness (mm), mass (g), and any acceptance measurement (e.g. grounding Ω per CS-010).

### 7.2 Freeze

1. **The stack freezes before mold machining starts.** Mold geometry (offsets, flange height) can depend on stack thickness, and machine slots are too scarce to cut twice (PP-03, PP-06). For mold-less flat panels, freeze before cloth is cut. Inside a run the app holds this order: Stack frozen is a blocker ahead of the mold steps. A mold advanced on the Molds tab or from the cut list is not checked against it, so the lead checks those at the weekly meeting (§8).
2. The freeze is the buy-off of the Stack frozen step on the run. Before signing, the signer shall have agreed the stack with the RE and the Composites Lead (plus the requesting subteam's lead for cross-team parts, the dashboard lesson, PP-04), and shall name them in the step's note. The app refuses to sign this step while the run has no plies; the note naming who agreed is this standard's requirement, not the app's.
3. Every stack-freeze date appears as a deadline in the Schedule tab, reviewed at the weekly meeting (PP-07). A frozen stack changes only by a run revision (steps 4 to 6): quick to do, but visible and counted.
4. Edit the run's stack in Stack & BOM. The run then shows how many plies differ from the part's plan.
5. Raise the run's Revision by one letter in its Details, and write why in the run's Notes.
6. If future runs should follow the change, press Compare on the run's stack, then Adopt as the part's plan.

### 7.4 Record as-built (during/after layup)

1. During layup, the RE checks each ply against the run's stack in Stack & BOM. Any change (substituted cloth, extra ply, mesh moved) is edited into the run's stack row by row, with the reason in that ply's Notes cell. The RE may also tick each ply in pen on the printed traveler. No deviation is too small to note: "we were out of 195 so used 220" is exactly the data that explains a mass or thickness surprise later.
2. Photograph the stack at: cores/mesh placed, and bagged-before-infusion. Photos attach to the WO steps.
3. After cure, record actual mass and thickness next to targets in the run's Quality section.
4. At the buy-off that starts the cure (Infuse, Wet layup and bag or Wet layup over core), confirm or correct Materials used for each BOM line. The app prefills each line from the plan; a blank means not measured, and stays that way in the record.
5. If this run proved a BOM line of the plan wrong, press ↩ plan on that line, so the next run starts from what this one learned. The button shows once a line's used quantity differs from the plan.
6. If this run proved the plan's stack wrong, press Compare on the run's stack, then Adopt as the part's plan.

### 7.5 Coupons: IDs and data files

A coupon is `CPN-SNx-###`: its own record on the app's R&D bench, belonging to a study `RDS-SNx-###` (CS-013 §4.1). The study's Materials carry the Layup stack (as text, in the §7.3 order from P1 outward, or the ID of the run whose panel the coupons were cut from), the Fabric lot, Resin lot and Hardener lot, the Lot record, Laid up on and Laid up by. Every coupon inherits them. A coupon that was different says so in its own row, and clearing that cell hands it back to the study. That inheritance is what makes one coupon ID enough to recover the whole build. (The retired suffix spelling `PNL-SNx-###-Cnn` survives only on frozen SN5-era records.)

1. **Write every coupon ID on the panel before the first cut**, across the cut lines, so each piece inherits a legible fragment. After the saw there is no way to recover which piece was which.
2. Name the raw data file after the coupon: `CPN-SN6-042.csv`. That one filename then carries the study, the stack, the session and the material lots, through one link each, none of them retyped.

> This is written against a real failure. The SN5 tensile set is seventeen CSVs named `TensileTesting_12_8_2025_<n>.csv` whose contents are Time, Displacement and Force with no header block and no specimen identity of any kind. The mapping from specimen to layup exists only in a Word table that contradicts itself. Untraceable test data is noise (§2).

## 8. Quality checks / acceptance criteria

| Check | Target | Method | Record where |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stack frozen before mold machining / cloth cut | Stack frozen signed, its note naming who agreed | Run blocker step in the app; for any mold advanced on the Molds tab or the cut list, lead check at the weekly meeting that its run shows Stack frozen signed (neither checks it) | Run (Stack frozen step) |
| As-built matches frozen stack | Any deviation noted, none silent | RE self-check + photo review | Run's Stack & BOM |
| Mass vs target | Within stated tolerance (default ±10%, team value) | Scale | The run's Quality section |
| Thickness vs target | Within stated tolerance | Calipers at 3+ points | The run's Quality section |

## 9. Records

The part's plan (Layup stack and Materials (plan)), each run's as-built stack and materials used with its deviations, and the step photos together are the permanent record. The Master-Tracker-style shorthand may still be used in dashboards/summaries, but it is a *view* of those records, never the source of truth.
