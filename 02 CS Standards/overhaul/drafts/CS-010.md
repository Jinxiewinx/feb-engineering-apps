## 1. Purpose

EV rules want conductive parts near HV/chassis grounded (our SN5 working criterion: <5 Ω to the grounding point). SN5 gave us both the success story and the cautionary tale. The diffuser (target and method written down up front, copper mesh in the stack) validated at <1 Ω continuous everywhere (2026-06-21) and drew praise from the design judges at comp. The catch can had no written criterion; its method was improvised for six months (Nov→May) and never hit target with CF wrapping, so we finally solved it by *giving up on CF for that geometry* and going to epoxy + copper paths (PP-05). This doc exists so we repeat the first one.

## 2. Scope

Any CF part with a grounding/bonding requirement, and any conductive-path retrofit on existing parts. The Ω target and rule interpretation come from the EV rules owner (powertrain/rules lead); this standard covers how composites *achieves and proves* the target, not what the target legally is.

Requests from other subteams (cooling, brakes, aero) arrive as a work order with the Other technique. The Ω and distance targets on them stay the rules owner's numbers.

## 4. Definitions

- **Grounding point**: the fastener/tab where the part's conductive path meets chassis ground.
- **Probe pattern**: the defined set of surface points measured against the grounding point.
- **CF as conductor**: carbon laminate conducts, but through-thickness resistance through resin-rich layers is high and unpredictable; bare CF also can't be reliably terminated. Hence copper mesh.
- **Surface path**: a conductor applied to the cured part's surface: copper strip or braid bonded with epoxy, copper tape, or conductive paint.

## 6. Safety

Grounding verification happens on or near the car: follow EV safety rules. No probing near energized HV; HV isolation confirmed by the ESO before resistance measurements on installed parts. Bench measurements on loose parts: no special hazards beyond CS-009 dust rules if sanding contact points.

Graphite powder (§5, §7.2 step 4): wear the respirator with a P100 cartridge and nitrile gloves while weighing and mixing it. The shop stocks A + P100 cartridges and nitrile gloves (`inventory.js` restock table). No graphite SDS is on file yet. Cut copper mesh has sharp wire ends: wear gloves when cutting and placing it.

## 7. Procedure

### 7.1 Decision point first (the catch-can lesson)

1. Before any layup work, decide whether CF with copper mesh is the right conductive path for this geometry. Write the answer and a one-line why in the note on "Stack frozen". A retrofit or a cross-team grounding job is created with the Other technique, and the answer goes in the note on "Define acceptance criterion: target and method, set before work starts", the step that blocks everything after it.
   - **Good fit:** large open laminates (diffuser, panels) where mesh lays flat in the stack and terminates at accessible flange points.
   - **Bad fit:** thin-walled, tapered, or wrapped metal substrates (the catch can: sanding punctured the can wall at the taper; through-thickness contact never stabilized). For these, use a surface path, or reconsider whether the part needs CF at all.
   - **Surface path:** copper strip or braid bonded with epoxy, copper tape, or conductive paint, applied after cure. Weigh mass as well as geometry: mesh in the stack is heavy on a large part like the undertray (R7).
2. Write the acceptance criterion before work starts: Ω target, probe pattern (grid spacing or named points), and measurement method (7.3). On the run, add a Quality row ("+ check" in edit mode) with Criterion "grounding resistance" and fill its Target when the run is created. On an Other run, write the same criterion in the note on "Define acceptance criterion: target and method, set before work starts". Six months of drift happened because none of this was written (R1).
3. A conductive path this team has not yet proven is proven on a first article by the §7.3 method before any part depends on it.

![Choosing the conductive path: mesh in the stack, a surface path, or no CF, decided by geometry, permeability in an infusion, mass, and access to the grounding point.](figures/cs010-path-decision.v2.png)

### 7.2 Build the path (mesh-in-stack method: the diffuser recipe)

1. Put copper mesh in the ply table per CS-002: mesh layer(s) positioned to be continuous from part surface zones to the grounding point(s), 25 mm minimum overlap (team value) where mesh sheets meet. Name the ply's material "copper mesh" so the stack view tags it Mesh. Only permeable mesh shall go in an infused stack: foil, solid sheet or a paint film blocks resin flow (R7).
2. Mesh must reach the grounding point as bare, terminable material: leave a mesh tail at the flange/fastener location, protected during infusion (peel-ply window or masked zone).
3. For surfaces needing exposed conductivity, an 88-gsm outer ply over mesh keeps finish while staying thin; validate on the first article that surface-to-ground still passes.
4. Graphite powder (1–5 wt% in the resin) may be added as a *supplement* in zones where mesh coverage is marginal, never as the primary path (SN5 trials judged it insufficient alone, R3). If used, record wt% on the WO.
5. Infuse/laminate per CS-006/CS-007. Note on the WO where mesh actually ended up (as-built).

### 7.3 Measure: one method, every time

1. Zero the multimeter leads; record lead resistance.
2. Fasten the grounding hardware as it will fly (torqued, washers as designed); hand-held contact readings are noise.
3. Probe every point in the run's probe pattern (default: 100 mm grid over conductive zones + every edge midpoint) to the grounding point. Record each value minus lead offset as a table in the note of the measuring step ("Trim and finish" on Mold infusion, Mold wet lay and Foam wrapped runs; "Finish" on a Glass infusion run; "Verify against criterion" on an Other run). Write the worst reading as the Actual on the "grounding resistance" Quality row.
4. Pass = every point ≤ target (default <5 Ω, or the rules value on the run). Not "one good point": that ambiguity burned a month in May 2026 (R1). A point over target is an issue: press ⚑ (Report an issue on this step) on the measuring step, and fix it per §7.4 before it is disposed.

### 7.4 If it fails

1. Map the failing zone; usually a mesh discontinuity or resin-isolated overlap.
2. Fixes in order of preference: expose + bond a copper strip bridge (epoxy + copper path), add an external ground tab at the failing zone, or lightly abrade to mesh at a termination point and re-terminate. Do not sand thin laminates chasing contact. That is how the catch cans died (R1).
3. Re-measure the full pattern after any fix, not just the failed point.

## 9. Records

The run keeps: the path decision and its one-line why (the "Stack frozen" note, or the criterion step's note on an Other run), the criterion (the "grounding resistance" Quality row), the full probe-reading table (values, not "pass") in the measuring step's note, photos of mesh placement and termination, and any retrofit fixes with their ⚑ issues.

WO-SN5-006 (UT diffuser) is the reference part: target set up front, method recorded, <1 Ω at comp on 2026-06-21 (R4, R5). Its record holds that summary only, not a probe table; the first SN6 grounded part will hold the first full one. WO-SN5-026 (catch can, about 40 Ω on 2026-05-25 against <5 Ω needed) is the counter-example (R6).
