## 1. Where do molds go?

Molds live in the RFS container, and never in front of the knaack or screw boxes (CS-011 §7.3, PP-10). Every mold points at its shelf through its Home location field, so "where is the seat mold" is a lookup, not a Slack ask.

Also called: mold storage, mould storage, find a mold, store a mold, put away a mold, RFS container, container, home location.

- SN5 molds blocked everyone's boxes at RFS (CS-011 §1). That is why the rule names the knaack and screw boxes.
- The monthly walk checks every mold is in its home location; the target is 100% (CS-011 §8).
- A mold that will not be used again is retired, never deleted (CS-003 §4). It still needs a place.
- Moving a big mold between sites: two people for a mold over about 1 m² (CS-011 §7.4 step 1; QR-014 §12).
- To record where you put it: scan the mold, tap Move, scan the shelf (CS-011 §7.3 step 3; QR-014 §8).

## 2. Where do finished parts go?

Finished parts go in the Jacobs basement, which needs keycard access. Never the etch locker: that was agreed on 2026-04-20 after parts were evicted from it (CS-011 §7.3, §1).

Also called: part storage, finished parts, find a part, store a part, Jacobs, Jacobs basement, Jacobs Hall, etch locker, keycard.

- Parts travel padded and labeled (CS-011 §7.4 step 2).
- Each part's label carries its record, and scanning it shows its Location (CS-001 §9).
- Record the shelf when you put a part away: scan the part, tap Move, scan the shelf (QR-014 §8).

## 3. Where do cloth, foam and tooling board go?

Cloth goes in the Dry sealed bin. Foam and tooling-board stock go in the RFS container, and tooling board is logged under Inventory → Boards, where the real board inventory lives day to day (CS-011 §7.3).

Also called: cloth, carbon cloth, fabric, roll, glass cloth, 195 twill; foam, core, Rohacell; tooling board, board, Precision Board, offcuts, Boards, Stock.

1. Put cloth rolls in the Dry sealed bin. No rule beyond the site (CS-011 §7.3).
2. Put foam and tooling-board stock in the RFS container.
3. Log each tooling board and usable offcut under Inventory → Boards (CS-011 §7.3 step 1).
4. Record the shelf on each cloth roll's lot with Move (QR-014 §8).

- One roll of cloth is one lot record, `FAB-SN6-###` (CS-014 §4).

## 4. Where are resin and hardener stored? Can they share a shelf?

Resins and hardeners go in the chemical boxes or barrel, never in a food fridge (CS-011 §6). No app Site names the boxes or the barrel yet (CS-011 §7.3). As written today, CS-011 §6 also says to segregate hardeners from resins on different shelves.

Co-storage: see CS-011 §6; under review.

Also called: resin storage, hardener storage, chemical storage, store epoxy, IN2, AT30, West, 105, 206, XCR, chemical box, barrel, can resin and hardener be stored together, same shelf, segregate.

- Keep every resin and hardener container closed (CS-011 §7.6).
- Temperatures and shelf life: IN2 and AT30 10–25 °C, XCR 20–25 °C, 12 months each by the datasheets (CS-011 §7.6; QR-014 §10).
- By their SDSs, the resins and hardeners are not flammable (CS-014 §6). Which cabinet they live in is CS-011 §6's call, not the Hazard field's (CS-014 §6).
- Put chemicals straight onto a location CS-011 §6 allows for them (CS-014 §6 step 3).
- Wear nitrile gloves to unpack and shelve chemical containers (CS-014 §6 step 1).
- A leaking container does not go on a shelf: leave it in its packaging, set it apart from other chemicals and tell a lead (CS-014 §6 step 2).

## 5. Where do flammables go? Acetone, IPA, Airtac, clear coat

Flammables go in the flammables cabinet: Airtac, acetone and clear coat by name (CS-011 §6). By their SDSs, Airtac 2, LiME LiNE Part A, acetone and 91% IPA are flammable; the epoxies are not (CS-014 §6).

Also called: flammables, flammables cabinet, flammable cabinet, acetone, IPA, isopropyl alcohol, Airtac, spray tack, clear coat, LiME LiNE, solvent storage.

| Material | Hazard by its SDS (CS-014 §6) |
|---|---|
| Airtac 2 spray | flammable |
| LiME LiNE UC4:1 Part A | flammable |
| Acetone | flammable |
| 91% IPA | flammable |
| IN2, AT30, XCR, West 105, 205, 206, 209 | not flammable |

IPA is flammable by its SDS too, but CS-011 §6 does not name it, and CS-004 §5 lists the 91% bottle in the Chemicals box. CS-011 §6 is under review.

In the app:

- The site is called Flammables cabinet. A storage location that may hold flammables has Rated for flammables set to Yes.
- Set Hazard on every chemical lot the day it arrives: open the lot's page, press Edit, set Hazard (CS-014 §6 step 4).
- A shelf card warns when it holds a flammable lot and is not a rated location.
- The Receiving desk warns "N flammable items would land on [shelf], which is not a rated location" only for a Material type whose restock rule a lead has marked flammable. None of the shipped rules is, so today the desk does not warn. Set Hazard on each lot from the SDS (CS-014 §6).

## 6. Can chemicals go in a fridge?

No. Resin, hardener and every other chemical never go in a fridge (CS-011 §6, CS-014 §6 step 3). The rule comes from the food-fridge incident of 2026-02-17, when chemicals ended up in a food fridge (PP-10).

Also called: fridge, refrigerator, cold storage, keep resin cold, food fridge, freezer.

- The monthly walk checks it: open every location whose Type is fridge and check it holds no resin, hardener or other chemical lot. The target is 0 (CS-011 §8).
- The app does not warn about a chemical on a fridge-type location. The walk is the only check (CS-011 §8).
- Storage temperatures and shelf life: QR-014 §10.

## 7. Where do consumables go? Bags, peel ply, cups, gloves

Consumables go in the General Box at RFS (CS-011 §7.3). Anything that fits no site goes under Other, and the location is named after the place.

Also called: consumables, consumables box, General Box, bag film, VB160, peel ply, flow mesh, tacky tape, cups, mixing cups, sticks, gloves, nitrile gloves, cartridges, breather.

- A consumable is one lot record with a count, `CON-SN6-###` (CS-014 §4).
- A Material type at or below its minimum shows on the Running out card on Inventory → Storage map (CS-011 §5).
- Minimums for the blocking consumables (CS-011 §5): VB160 bag film 1 roll, peel ply 1 roll, flow mesh 1 roll, tacky tape 6 rolls, nitrile gloves 2 boxes per size, mixing cups 50, mixing sticks 50, respirator cartridges (A + P100) 2 sets.
- Reordering from the Running out card: QR-015.

## 8. Moved a mold or part to another shelf? Recording the move

Scan the object, tap Move, scan the shelf (CS-011 §7.3 step 3). To put several things on one shelf, open the shelf and use Move here (scan).

Also called: move, moving, moved, relocate, put away, record a move, change location, scan, Move here, location, home location.

One thing:

1. Scan the object's label, or open its record.
2. Tap Move.
3. Tap Scan the shelf and scan the shelf's label. The move saves as soon as the label reads ("Moved to …"). Or pick the shelf from Location and press Move it.

Several things onto one shelf:

1. Open the shelf on Inventory → Storage map.
2. Press Move here (scan).
3. Scan each object's label.

- The location is a controlled value: every shelf, bin and rack is its own record, so the walk checks reality against it instead of whatever string somebody typed (CS-011 §7.3).
- Something on the wrong shelf during the walk: fix it with Move as you go (CS-011 §7.1 step 3).

## 9. How do I add a new shelf, bin or rack?

Press + Location on Inventory → Storage map, set its Site from the storage table, and put its printed label on the shelf's front edge (CS-011 §7.3 step 2, CS-001 §7.10). Every mold, part and material lot then points at a storage record.

Also called: new shelf, add a shelf, new bin, new location, storage location, BIN, rack, cabinet, shelf label, + Location.

1. Open Inventory → Storage map.
2. Press + Location.
3. Name it after the place.
4. Set Site: RFS container, Jacobs basement, Flammables cabinet, Dry sealed bin, General Box or Other.
5. Set Type (shelf, rack, cabinet, bin, box or fridge).
6. If it is rated for flammables, set Rated for flammables to Yes.
7. If campus EH&S has stuck an RSS sublocation tag on it, record that in EH&S barcode (CS-014 §4).
8. Print its label and stick it on the front edge (CS-001 §7.10).

The site names are the ones the app uses (Site on a storage location). CS-011 §7.3 notes they still need a field check against the real shelves.

A storage location's ID is `BIN-SN6-###` (CS-013 §4.1), and its label's key line reads STORAGE (CS-001 §4).

## 10. What temperature does resin need, and how long does it keep?

IN2 and AT30: 10–25 °C (50–77 °F), tightly sealed, 12 months. XCR: 20–25 °C (68–77 °F), tightly sealed, 12 months. West 105 and its hardeners: room temperature, containers closed, usable for many years (CS-011 §7.6, datasheet figures).

Also called: storage temperature, shelf life, expiry, expiration, how long does resin last, out of date, cold resin, crystallized, crystallised, cloudy 105.

| Material | Store at | Shelf life | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| IN2 resin and AT30 hardener | 10–25 °C (50–77 °F), tightly sealed | 12 months when stored correctly | `03 Datasheets/EC-TDS-IN2-Infusion-Resin.pdf` |
| XCR resin and hardener | 20–25 °C (68–77 °F), tightly sealed | 12 months when stored correctly | `03 Datasheets/EC-TDS-XCR-Epoxy-Coating-Resin.pdf` |
| West 105 with 205, 206 or 209 | Room temperature, containers closed | Many years with proper storage | West TDSs (CS-011 §7.6) |

- These are datasheet figures, not team holds (CS-011 §7.6).
- Do not let West 105 freeze and thaw repeatedly: it crystallizes. Crystallized 105 is warmed to 125 °F and stirred until clear before use (CS-008 §7 step 8).
- After long storage, check the West pumps' metering and mix a small test batch to confirm it cures (CS-011 §7.6 step 2).
- Check the expiry before you mix; past it, ask a lead (CS-008 §7 step 1).

## 11. How do I check what is on a shelf? The monthly stock walk

Open Inventory → Storage map, open each shelf's card, check the list against what is on the shelf, and press Confirm contents (CS-011 §7.1). The walk is monthly and takes about 15 minutes (team estimate).

Also called: stock walk, inventory check, stock check, stocktake, audit, Confirm contents, need a walk, Running out, what do we have.

1. Open Inventory → Storage map.
2. Open a shelf's card and check its list against the shelf.
3. Press Confirm contents (Confirm on the map card does the same).
4. Fix what is wrong as you go: Move, How full, or the shelf's Add here card for anything missing.
5. Open the Running out card. For any row without "on order", press Add N to a purchase.
6. Post the purchase request in #purchasing-slackbot the same day (CS-011 §7.1 step 5).

- The lead names the stock-walk owner at the start of the season (CS-011 §7.1 step 1).
- The "need a walk" chip counts shelves not confirmed in 30 days; the target is 0 (CS-011 §8).
- A shelf's page folds identical containers into one row by Material type (CS-011 §7.3).
- Mark an emptied container Empty rather than deleting it (CS-011 §7.1).

## 12. Moving a big mold or a part between sites

Molds over about 1 m² take two people and a vehicle that actually fits them: plan it, don't discover it (CS-011 §7.4 step 1). Parts travel padded and labeled (CS-011 §7.4 step 2). The SN5 budget shows a U-Haul was needed for mold moves.

Also called: transport, moving molds, carrying a mold, van, U-Haul, car, trip to RFS, taking parts to Jacobs.

1. Plan the move ahead. A mold over about 1 m² needs two people.
2. Use a vehicle the mold actually fits in.
3. Pad and label parts before they travel.
4. At the other end, record the move: scan the object, tap Move, scan the shelf (QR-014 §8).

Heavy tooling-board stacks: two people to move anything over about half a sheet, and watch pinch points (CS-003 §6).
