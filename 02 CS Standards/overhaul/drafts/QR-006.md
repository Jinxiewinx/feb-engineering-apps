## 1. How do I log a delivery?

On Inventory, press Receive a delivery to open the Receiving desk. Set Supplier and Arrived, add one row per kind of thing, press Review N records, then Create N records. Log it the day it arrives (CS-014 §8).

Also called: receive, receiving, delivery, package, box, order arrived, log a delivery, check in stock.

1. Open the desk: Inventory → Storage map → Receive a delivery. From a line on the Incoming card, Arrived ▸ fills the whole order in for you (CS-014 §7.2).
2. If the desk shows "In the last hour: [name] added N. Same delivery?", ask that person before you type anything.
3. Set Supplier.
4. Set Arrived to the day the delivery came in. It becomes every record's Received date.
5. Set New lines land on to the shelf most of the delivery goes to.
6. Start a row and pick its Class first: Fabric, Resin, Hardener or Consumable.
7. Put a kit's resin and hardener on separate rows, Resin and Hardener.
8. Type What is it as the container names it, and How many.
9. Set Shelf on any row going somewhere other than the default.
10. Type Vendor lot exactly as printed, or leave it blank if nothing is printed.
11. Fill EH&S tag and Expires (QR-006 §3 and §5).
12. Type $ each, the price of one unit.
13. Press Enter to start the next row.
14. Press Review N records, check the list and its warnings, then press Create N records (QR-006 §6).

If you leave before finishing, the sheet is kept on this device for 24 hours and Inventory shows "Finish N lines" (CS-014 §7.2 step 4). It is not shared with anyone else's device.

Tooling board does not go through the desk. Log it under Inventory → Boards (CS-014 §2).

## 2. One record per roll or jug: how many records will a row make?

Fabric, Resin and Hardener rows make one record per roll or container: How many 3 makes 3 records. A Consumable row makes one record carrying the count, "1 of 50" (CS-014 §4; §7.3 step 7).

Also called: lot, batch, jug, roll, container, one record each, how many records, count.

A lot is one record for one physical thing on a shelf (CS-014 §4). The IDs are `FAB-SN6-###` for fabric, `RSN-SN6-###` for resin and hardener (the record's Role says which), and `CON-SN6-###` for consumables, each with its own counter.

The desk refuses more than 50 tracked units on one row (CS-014 §7.3 step 7). Split the row, or log a box of small items as one Consumable with a count.

Why one per jug: the cure buy-off asks which resin lot and which hardener lot went into the part, and pre-fills the most recently opened lot of each class (CS-014 §7.8). Two jugs on one record make that answer meaningless.

A row with no shelf lands in No location on the storage map. Put it away the same day (CS-014 §7.3 step 8).

If you paste a block copied from an order email, each line becomes a row. Check the Class, How many and Shelf of every pasted row (CS-014 §7.3 step 13).

## 3. Where do the lot number and expiry date come from?

Vendor lot: type it exactly as printed on the container or slip, blank if none. Expires: type it off the container. Fabric has no expiry field. The lot's Expiry from field says where a date came from (CS-014 §4; §7.3 step 9; §7.7).

Also called: lot number, batch number, vendor lot; expiry, expiration, expiry date, use-by, best before, shelf life.

Expiry from has three values (CS-014 §4):

| Expiry from | Meaning |
|---|---|
| vendor label | A person typed it |
| vendor label (AI read) | A ✨ read set it and nobody has typed over it |
| shelf-life table | Link materials worked it out from Received plus the TDS shelf life |

1. At the desk, type Expires off the container for every resin, hardener and chemical consumable that prints one (CS-014 §7.7 step 1).
2. If the container prints only a manufacture date and a shelf life, leave Expires blank (step 2).
3. Later, on the Materials list, press Link materials. It fills a blank Expires from Received plus the TDS shelf life and never changes a filled field (step 3).
4. Where the label gives a date, type it over a shelf-life date. A shelf-life date counts from the day we received the container, so it can run later than the maker's date (step 4).

The shelf lives in the table are datasheet figures: IN2 and AT30 12 months (`03 Datasheets/EC-TDS-IN2-Infusion-Resin.pdf`), XCR 12 months (`03 Datasheets/EC-TDS-XCR-Epoxy-Coating-Resin.pdf`), when stored as the TDS says (CS-014 §7.7 step 3).

To read a container's label with the camera, open the lot's page and press ✨ Read label. In the "From the label" sheet, untick anything that does not match the container, then press Apply (CS-014 §7.7 steps 5 and 6).

## 4. What does ✨ From packing slip do, and why check every row?

It reads a photo or PDF of the slip and adds one row per shipped line: name, quantity shipped, and vendor lot and expiry only where the slip prints them. Anything a ✨ button fills is a draft: the person saving it shall check every row against the paper (CS-014 §7.4).

Also called: packing slip, delivery note, AI read, AI fill, sparkle button, scan the slip, read the slip.

The button shows under the sheet only when a lead has AI features on. Class, Material type, supplier and cost come from the app's own tables, never from the model. An expiry printed as month and year is read as the last day of that month (CS-014 §7.4).

1. Press ✨ From packing slip and photograph the slip, or pick its PDF.
2. Wait for "N lines read from the slip. Check the class, the shelf and the count on each."
3. Check every row against the slip and against what is physically in the box: Class, What is it, How many, Vendor lot, Expires.
4. Fix a wrong cell by typing in it.
5. Remove any row for something not in the box with the bin icon (Remove this line).
6. Where the container prints an expiry, type it into Expires from the container. That clears the AI mark.
7. If the read fails or finds no lines, type the rows or paste them from the order email.

An AI-read expiry stays marked "vendor label (AI read)" until a person types over it, and an AI read is never a source for a number in a standard (CS-014 §7.4). The photo is deleted as soon as it is read (CS-014 §5).

Each person gets 50 photo reads a day, shared by receipts, packing slips and container labels (CS-014 §5).

## 5. Recording the EH&S tag when a delivery arrives

For each Resin, Hardener or chemical Consumable row, press the camera button in the EH&S tag cell and scan the tag on every container in the row. One code per container: the number of codes on a row equals How many (CS-014 §7.6).

Also called: EH&S tag, UC tag, RSS tag, campus barcode, chemical barcode, Data Matrix.

1. Press the camera button in the row's EH&S tag cell. The camera stays open; each scan adds a code.
2. Scan the tag on each container in the row.
3. If the camera fails, type all 24 characters from the face of the tag. Separate several codes with spaces.
4. Do not record a new container by its 12-character edge print. Typed at receipt, it saves a short code that will not match the RSS inventory (CS-014 §7.6 step 3).
5. Count the codes against How many. One code on a multi-container row raises no warning, so count them yourself (step 4).
6. Count each code's characters against the tag. The desk does not check length, and a short code saves anyway (step 6).
7. If the confirm says a tag is already on another record, that container is already tracked. Take it off the sheet (step 5).

A chemical container with no tag yet: leave the cell blank and give it a 5522 FEB label. When campus tags it, record the tag on its record and leave the FEB label on (CS-014 §7.6 step 7).

Cups, gloves, bag film and other non-chemical consumables have no tag. Leave the cell blank (step 8).

Tagged containers get no FEB label: the tag is the label (CS-001 §7.2; QR-005).

## 6. Confirm, print labels, or Undo

Press Review N records (or Ctrl+Enter), untick anything that did not turn up, read every warning, then press Create N records. The bar "N records added" offers Print N labels and Undo. Labels are never printed automatically (CS-014 §7.5).

Also called: confirm, create records, print labels, undo receiving, review.

1. Press Review N records. The confirm "Create N records?" lists the rows by shelf.
2. Untick anything that did not actually turn up.
3. Read every warning: EH&S tag counts that do not fit, a tag already on another record, flammable items landing on a shelf not rated for them. Fix them with Back to the sheet.
4. Press Create N records. Every record starts Sealed, dated with Arrived.
5. If the batch has no EH&S-tagged containers, press Print N labels.
6. If it has tagged containers, print from Inventory → Labels instead, with What set to Material lots.
7. Tick only the records without a tag and press Preview N labels.
8. If something is wrong, press Undo. It deletes the records just made and puts the rows back on the sheet. Bin any labels already printed for them.

With no connection the app cannot get IDs, writes nothing and says so. Try again when back online (CS-014 §7.5 step 4).

When every line of a purchase is in, set its Order status to Arrived on the Budget tab. Receiving does not change it (CS-014 §7.5 step 11).

## 7. Setting Hazard: is it flammable?

The receiver shall set Hazard on every resin, hardener and chemical consumable lot the day it is received: open the lot's page, press Edit, set Hazard. Receiving does not set it yet (CS-014 §6 step 4).

Also called: hazard, flammable, flammables, not flammable, flash point, SDS, fire risk.

Read it off the SDS §2. Pick "flammable" when the SDS lists a flammable gas, aerosol or liquid (GHS H220 to H226, or an older OSHA Class I flammable liquid), otherwise "not flammable" (CS-014 §6 step 4). Blank means unknown, and an unknown lot cannot trigger the storage map's flammables warning (CS-014 §4).

| Material | Hazard (CS-014 §6, from the SDS) |
|---|---|
| IN2 resin, AT30 SLOW, AT30 FAST | not flammable |
| XCR resin and hardener | not flammable |
| West 105 resin; 205, 206, 209 hardeners | not flammable |
| Airtac 2 spray | flammable |
| LiME LiNE UC4:1 Part A | flammable |
| Acetone | flammable |
| 91% IPA | flammable |

Hazard records what the SDS says. Which cabinet a material lives in is CS-011 §6's call (CS-014 §6). Co-storage: see CS-011 §6; under review.

The desk's confirm can warn that flammable items would land on a location that is not rated. That warning comes from the app's restock table, which still marks the epoxies flammable. Treat it as a prompt to check CS-011 §6, and set Hazard from the SDS as above (CS-014 §6).

## 8. Damaged or leaking container at delivery

Wear nitrile gloves when you unpack and shelve chemical containers. Do not receive a leaking container onto a shelf: leave it in its packaging, set it apart from other chemicals, read its SDS and tell a lead (CS-014 §6 steps 1 and 2).

Also called: leaking, damaged, broken jug, spill, cracked container, leaking delivery.

Chemicals go straight onto a location CS-011 §6 allows for them, never a fridge (CS-014 §6 step 3). Nitrile gloves are shop stock (CS-014 §5).

## 9. Part of the order came, or donated or sponsor stock

Receive only what came; the rest stays on Incoming as N of M in. Donated, sponsor or legacy stock goes through Receive a delivery with the donor in Supplier and $ each blank (CS-014 §7.5 step 10; §7.11).

Also called: donated, donor, donation, sponsor stock, legacy stock, partial delivery, part of the order, short shipped, backorder.

Part of the order came:

1. Receive only what came (CS-014 §7.5 step 10).
2. The rest stays on Incoming as "N of M in · K to come".
3. If a line will never arrive, tell a lead. The app has no button to close a line short yet (CS-014 §7.1 step 4).

Donated, sponsor or legacy stock (CS-014 §7.11):

1. Receive it through the desk from Receive a delivery, like any delivery. There is no purchase, so no Arrived ▸.
2. Name the sponsor or donor in Supplier.
3. Leave $ each blank. Donated stock stays un-costed.
4. For legacy stock found on a shelf with no record, set Arrived to the day you log it.
5. Set Hazard, the EH&S tag and Expires on chemical containers as in QR-006 §3, §5 and §7.
