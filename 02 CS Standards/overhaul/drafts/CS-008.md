## 1. Purpose

One table for every resin we stock: ratios, pot lives, cure/demould times, plus the mixing discipline and exotherm rules that apply to all of them. Exists because of soft-spot failures (bad ratios), the first forged-CF part cracking from resin starvation, and a few too many hot cups.

## 2. Scope

All team resin systems. Process-specific use lives in CS-004 (XCR), CS-006 (IN2), CS-007 (West 105).

## 4. Definitions

- **Pot life**: usable time in the mixing pot, at the mass and temperature the TDS states it for. Bigger batch or warmer shop means shorter; the quoted figure is the ceiling, not a promise.
- **Gel**: the resin no longer flows. Gel is not cure: a gelled part pried off a mold drags resin with it.
- **Demould**: the earliest the part can leave the mold without damage. Two columns in §5: the datasheet cure figure and the FEB hold that governs (§5.1).
- **Full cure**: full mechanical properties. Weeks, not hours, for every system we stock; no structural or fit loading before then where avoidable.
- **Exotherm**: the cure reaction's own heat. Mass concentrated in a cup accelerates it; the runaway case is §6's hot-cup rule.

### 5.1 The two demould columns, and which one is the rule

**Datasheet cure figure** is what the manufacturer publishes, at the temperature
they publish it at: a demould time for IN2, a thin-film cure for West, an
initial cure for XCR. Every figure is quoted from a TDS in `03 Datasheets/`.

**FEB hold** is what this team waits, and it is the one that governs. It is
longer than the datasheet in every row, on purpose, and it is a team decision
rather than a manufacturer claim, so it is never presented as one. Signed off
by Simon Starbuck, 2026-08-01.

The holds are shift boundaries, not multiples of the datasheet: 24 h means "come
back tomorrow" and 48 h means "come back after the weekend". A hold that expires
at 3 am helps nobody, because nobody is at RFS at 3 am. That is why the ratios
against the datasheet look uneven (1.3× for XCR, 3× for the FAST systems); they
were never derived from it.

Two consequences worth knowing before you pick a hardener:

- **Both FAST systems hold 24 h.** Buying FAST hardener to turn two infusions
  round in one day does not work under this rule. That is deliberate: we have no
  record of a part ruined by waiting and several of work being rushed. But it is
  the line to revisit if the trade stops being worth it.
- **A cold shop is not covered by these numbers.** Every figure assumes the
  reference temperature in its row. We cure ambient with no oven, and RFS in
  January is not 25 °C. Log the temperature per §7 step 5 and fingernail-test the
  flange (§8) rather than trusting the clock. When the recorded shop temperature
  is below the datasheet's reference (25 °C for IN2, 22 °C for West, 20 °C for
  XCR, from `resins.js`), the app warns "Colder than this number assumes, so it
  will run long. Test the flange, not the clock." It leaves the hold unchanged.

![Datasheet cure figure (thin solid bar) against the FEB hold that governs (hollow bar), per system, on one time axis. The holds land on shift boundaries, which is why both FAST systems still hold 24 h.](figures/cs008-holds.v2.png)

**What the app enforces.** Signing the step that starts a cure ("Infuse", "Wet
layup and bag", "Wet layup over core") opens a buy-off that asks for "Resin
mixed", "Finished on" and "at", and "Shop temperature, °C (optional)". The next
step ("Cure and demould", or "Cure" on a Foam wrapped run) stays locked until
the FEB hold for that resin has run from the finish time. A member who tries to
sign it early is turned away. A lead may sign it early through "Demould early?",
which asks "Why are you demoulding early?" and writes the reason, the lead's
name and how many hours short it was to the work order's event log.

**What the app does not lock.** Mold sealing with XCR: no work-order step starts
an XCR cure, so its 12 h hold is watched by hand (CS-004 §7.3). Any cure
recorded as "Something else / not recorded": no resin means no hold. Retro
work orders, the records of SN5 runs, enforce nothing.

**Changing a hold.** The §5 table is the code table in
`06 Composites App/app/resins.js` at this revision, signed off 2026-08-01. The
live value is the app's. A lead may change a hold from the app: on a held step,
press "why N h?" to open "Why N hours?", press "Change this hold…", enter
"FEB hold (hours)", pick who signed it off and the date under "Signed off by",
and press "Save". The app refuses a hold below the datasheet figure and a hold
nobody signed off, and the change reaches every work order at once. "Revert to
the code table" in the same window undoes it. "Why N hours?" always shows the
hold in force and who signed it, and says when a lead set it from the app. A
lead who changes a hold shall open a revision of this standard within two weeks,
so the §5 table and the app agree again (CS-000 §2). A code-table hold shorter
than its datasheet figure fails the app's tests.

## 6. Safety: exotherm and sensitization (applies to every system)

| Product | Hazard statements for people (SDS §2, R4; aquatic hazards left out) |
|---|---|
| IN2 resin | H315 skin irritation, H317 allergic skin reaction, H319 serious eye irritation, H360F may damage fertility |
| AT30 SLOW hardener | H302 harmful if swallowed, H314 severe skin burns and eye damage, H317, H318 serious eye damage |
| AT30 FAST hardener | H302, H314, H317, H318, H373 organ damage through repeated exposure, EUH071 corrosive to the respiratory tract |
| XCR resin | H302, H315, H317, H318, H341 suspected of causing genetic defects |
| XCR hardener | H302, H314, H317, H318 |
| West 105 resin | H315, H317, H319 |
| West 205 fast hardener | H302, H312 harmful in contact with skin, H314, H317, H318, H341, H373 |
| West 206 slow hardener | H302, H314, H317, H318, H335 respiratory irritation |
| West 209 extra slow hardener | H302, H314, H317, H318, H335, H373 |

- Every resin and hardener here can make you allergic through the skin (H317). None is a respiratory sensitizer: no SDS on file carries H334. Every hardener burns skin and can blind (H314, H318). PPE: nitrile gloves; safety glasses with side shields (EN 166); ventilation; wash skin with soap, not solvent. For long or enclosed sessions you should wear a respirator: A1 cartridges cover XCR (XCR SDS §8.2), and AT30 work wants ABEK (AT30 SDS §8.2); ABEK and EN 166 goggles for XCR are on the purchase list.
- Resin or hardener in an eye: rinse with plenty of water, under the eyelids too, for at least 15 minutes, then get medical attention (R4: IN2 and AT30 SDS §4; West 205 SDS §4).
- **Exotherm rules:** mix the smallest batch that does the job; pour out of the pot promptly (mass concentrated in a cup accelerates); a warm cup = use immediately or spread thin; a hot/smoking cup = carry outside onto concrete and leave it. Do not bin, do not water-dilute indoors. Nothing goes in the trash until fully cured and cool.
- Never mix off-ratio to "speed up" or "slow down" cure; off-ratio = uncured sticky part. For IN2 you may blend AT30 FAST and SLOW instead, keeping the total at 100:30 by weight (R1). For West, no datasheet on file covers blending 205 and 206: do not blend them until one does.

## 7. Procedure

1. Pick the system from §5 by process standard; confirm pot life fits the job + a margin. You should check the expiry on the jug's label (the EXP date) or the Expires date on its lot page before you mix. Past it, ask a lead before it goes on a part.
   ⚠ 209 uses **3:1 pumps**, not the 5:1 pumps used by 205/206. Check the pump head before stroking; this is the classic wrong-ratio trap.
2. Weigh (scale, 1 g) or pump (West, full strokes only); record masses on the WO.
3. Mix ≥2 min scraping walls/base; **double-pot** (transfer + remix, fresh stick) for anything going on a part surface.
4. Forged CF mix math (R5): carbon mass ≈ mold volume × 1.4 g/cc; resin ≈ fiber × 1.25. Starving the mix made the first part brittle (failed bend test); don't eyeball it.
5. Cure at ≥20 °C wherever possible; log ambient temp on the WO when it isn't (cold cures run long; see each TDS).
6. Do not demould before the **FEB hold** in §5 has elapsed, counted from the end of the layup or infusion. The work order enforces this and records the resin, the finish time and the shop temperature; it is a lead call to go early, and the reason is logged. Gel is not cure: fingernail-test the flange (§8) before you trust either the clock or the feel of the part. When you sign the layup or infusion, pick the resin you actually mixed under "Resin mixed". Choose "Something else / not recorded" only for a resin not in §5; then write the resin in the step note, and its demould time is a lead's call.
7. Leftovers: spread thin on scrap or leave outside to cure; label per CS-001.
8. Cloudy or crystallized 105 after a cold spell: warm it to 125 °F and stir until clear before it goes in the pump (R3).
9. After a long gap between uses, mix a small test batch to check the West pumps meter correctly and the batch cures, before a part depends on them (R3).

## 9. Records

Batch masses, system + hardener speed, ambient temp, cure duration: on every WO that consumes resin. The resin, the finish time and the shop temperature are captured by the work order at the layup or infusion buy-off, so this is a byproduct of running the job rather than a separate write-up.

The same buy-off records "Which lots went in" (Fabric, Resin, Hardener, pre-filled with the open lot; "I don't know / not recorded" is an honest answer) and "Materials used", pre-filled from the plan, one line per BOM item. Correct any line that differs; a blank means not measured.
