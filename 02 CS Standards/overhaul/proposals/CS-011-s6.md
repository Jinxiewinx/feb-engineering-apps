## Short version

1. **Co-storage (R-011-01).** §6 says hardeners and resins go on different
   shelves. The shop keeps them together in the flammables cabinet, which is
   how campus EH&S filed them, and the app stopped warning about it on
   2026-08-28 on your decision, waiting for a Rev D that never carried the
   change. Recommendation: allow it, with each system in its own tray and
   nothing open or decanted beside its partner (option B below).
2. **Flammables list (R-011-02).** The list in §6 is right (Airtac, acetone,
   clear coat). What is wrong is around it: the app calls all five epoxies
   flammable and their SDSs say they are not. Recommendation: replace the
   parenthesis with a table that says, per material, what its SDS says and
   where it lives, and says plainly that the epoxies are in the cabinet
   because EH&S filed them there.
3. **One finding the audit missed.** Both AT30 SDSs say "Keep away from
   isocyanates". The clear coat's activator (LiME LiNE Part B) is an
   isocyanate and would live in the same flammables cabinet. Whatever you
   decide on 1, the amine hardeners and that can should not share a tray.
4. **Expired material (R-014-07).** No rule exists. Recommendation: the
   candidate wording in the last section, after Nick confirms the RFS
   disposal route.

## 1. Co-storage of resin and hardener (R-011-01)

### Current text (CS-011 Rev D, §6)

> Chemical storage per each SDS (R4): resins/hardeners in the chemical
> boxes/barrel, **never in food fridges** (2026-02-17 incident); flammables
> (Airtac, acetone, clear coat) in the flammables cabinet; segregate
> hardeners from resins on different shelves.

### How it got here

- 2026-08-28, commit `de7994a`: the "resin + hardener together" warning was
  removed from the storage map (`invLocWarnings`) and from the receiving
  confirm (`rxProposedWarnings`), recorded as "lead decision 2026-08-28".
  The commit says "CODE ONLY: CS-011 §6 still says separate shelves; Simon
  revises the standard himself at Rev D". Four tests were rewritten to assert
  the warning is absent.
- The same day CS-011 went to Rev D, whose history row says "No rule
  changed". The revision the code was waiting for never carried the change.
- SESSION-STATE still says "CS-011 §6 still forbids resin and hardener
  co-storage on purpose ... Simon revises the standard himself", so no
  session has touched it.
- Paul quotes §6 as the rule today, so a member asking "can I put the AT30
  next to the IN2?" is told no, while every shelf in the cabinet says yes.

### What the app does now

- No warning anywhere for resin and hardener on the same shelf
  (`app/inventory.js` invLocWarnings, comment at lines 387-393;
  `app/receiving.js` rxProposedWarnings). The comment says not to reintroduce
  the check unless the standard and the code change together.
- Campus RSS files the team's containers under one sublocation, "Formula
  Electric at Berkeley - Flammable Cabinet", with resin and hardener together
  (`app/ehsimport.js` header comment; the lead decision comment at
  `app/inventory.js` 387-393). The production import has not been run yet
  (SESSION-STATE).
- The app still records Role (resin or hardener) on every RSN lot. That is
  for the cure buy-off's lot pickers, not for storage.

### What the SDSs say

|---|---|---|---|

| IN2 resin | §10.3 Hazardous reactions | "Reacts with ... Bases, Strong oxidizing agents. Avoid amines." | same file, IN2 section |

| XCR resin | §7.2 and §10.5 | Incompatibilities: "No data available." Store in the original container, dry, cool, away from heat. Avoid frost | `03 Datasheets/EC-SDS-XCR-Epoxy-Coating-Resin-Combined.pdf` |
| XCR hardener | §10.5 | "Keep away from: strong oxidising agents." Keep container tightly closed; avoid humidity | same file |

Reading of it (mine, not the SDS's): one SDS, IN2's, asks for resin to be
kept away from amines, and every hardener in the shop is an amine (AT30,
XCR and West by their §3 compositions). The other five sheets say nothing
against co-storage. A sealed jug beside a sealed jug does not react. What the
IN2 line guards against is the two meeting: a leak, a drip down the side of a
jug, a spill on a shared shelf, which would then cure in place and can get
hot. A tray per system answers that without a second cabinet.

### Options

| Option | §6 says | What changes in the shop | What changes in the app | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Keep the rule | Separate shelves, as now | Move every hardener to a different shelf from its resin. Inside one cabinet that means hardeners on one shelf, resins on another | Put the warning back in invLocWarnings and rxProposedWarnings and re-flip four tests | Matches the IN2 SDS line literally | Fights the campus filing; the shop does not work this way; a rule nobody follows teaches people to ignore §6, including its fridge and flammables lines |
| B. Share, with trays (recommended) | May share a cabinet; sealed originals; each system in its own tray; nothing open or decanted beside its partner | Buy a tray or bin per system (IN2/AT30, XCR, West). Cost not in the repo; needs a quote | None. The comments that say "pending Simon's Rev D" get updated | Matches the shop, the campus filing and the app; answers the IN2 line's real risk (a leak reaching the other part) | Not the literal SDS wording; trays to buy and keep clean |
| C. Share freely | Resin and hardener may share a shelf | None | None | Simplest; exactly the shop today | Ignores the one SDS line that speaks to it; a leaking hardener jug can reach the resin |

If you read the IN2 line as advice for bulk warehouse storage, C is
defensible. B costs a few trays and leaves nothing to argue about at an EH&S
walk.

### Proposed wording (option B)

To replace the last clause of §6 ("segregate hardeners from resins on
different shelves"):

> Resin and hardener may share a cabinet, matching the campus EH&S filing,
> in their original containers with the lids tight. Keep each system's resin
> and hardener in its own tray or bin on the shelf, so a leak from one cannot
> reach the other. Never store an open or decanted container next to its
> partner. Keep the amine hardeners (AT30, XCR, West 205, 206, 209) away from
> isocyanates, which in this shop means the clear coat's activator (AT30
> SDS §7.2).

This is a process-rule change (a "shall" becomes a "may" with conditions), so
it needs its own line in the CS-011 revision history and in the register's
process-rule table.

## 2. The flammables list (R-011-02)

### Current text

Same §6 sentence: "flammables (Airtac, acetone, clear coat) in the
flammables cabinet". The list itself is right. It leaves out 91% IPA, and it
does not say why the epoxies are in the cabinet too.

### What the app does now

- The restock table marks IN2, AT30, West 105, West 206 and XCR
  `hazard: "flammable"` (`app/inventory.js` RESTOCK_SEED, lines 51-63).
- The receiving confirm uses that table to warn "N flammable items would land
  on [shelf], which is not a rated location" (`app/receiving.js`
  rxProposedWarnings).
- The EH&S import sets Hazard from the GHS H-codes in the RSS export:
  H220 to H226 means flammable (`app/ehsimport.js` ehsHazard). Under that test
  every epoxy comes out "not flammable". The two code paths disagree.
- Receiving does not write Hazard on a received lot at all (R-OTH-24), so a
  can of acetone received onto an unrated shelf raises nothing on the map
  afterwards. CS-014 §6 step 4 now tells the receiver to set it by hand, from
  the SDS.
- R-OTH-22 (decided "do") drops "flammable" from the five epoxies in the
  restock table. After that, the receiving warning fires for Airtac, acetone
  and the clear coat only, which is when it should.

### What the SDSs say

| Material | Flash point and class (SDS §2 and §9) | File in `03 Datasheets/` |
|---|---|---|
| Airtac 2 spray | Class IA flammable liquid, flash point −42 °F; flammable aerosol | `airtac2spray.pdf` |
| LiME LiNE UC4:1 Part A | Flam. Liq. 2 (H225); flash point below −18 °C | `LIMELINE-SDS-UC4-1-Clearcoat-Part-A.pdf` |
| LiME LiNE Part B (activator) | SDS not on file; an isocyanate | none yet (R-009-03) |
| Acetone | Flammable liquid, Category 2 | `SDS-Acetone-Fisher-A18.pdf` (stand-in; no number read from it) |
| 91% IPA | H225, highly flammable liquid | `SDS-Isopropyl-Alcohol-91-Alliance.pdf` (stand-in; no number read from it) |
| IN2 resin | Flash point 140 °C; no flammable class | `EC-SDS-IN2-Epoxy-Infusion-Resin-and-Hardener.pdf` |
| AT30 SLOW | 150 °C; no flammable class | same file |
| AT30 FAST | 100 °C; no flammable class | same file |
| XCR resin and hardener | Above 100 °C; no flammable class | `EC-SDS-XCR-Epoxy-Coating-Resin-Combined.pdf` |
| West 105 | Above 205 °F (96 °C), "estimated based on ingredient data" | `WEST-SDS-105-Epoxy-Resin.pdf` |
| West 205, 206, 209 | Above 200 °F (93 °C), estimated | the three West hardener SDSs |

The West figures are estimates, not measurements, and sit closest to the
line of anything here. None of the epoxy SDSs carries an H22x code.

### Proposed wording

Replace the whole of §6 with this. The fridge rule and the "per each SDS"
opening are kept; "chemical boxes/barrel" waits on open question 15 and is
left out until someone confirms it still exists.

> Chemical storage follows each SDS (R4). Never store a chemical in a food
> fridge (2026-02-17 incident, PP-10).
>
> | Material | What its SDS says | Store in |
> |---|---|---|
> | Airtac 2 spray | Class IA flammable liquid, flash point −42 °F (`airtac2spray.pdf`) | Flammables cabinet |
> | Acetone | Flammable liquid, Category 2 (`SDS-Acetone-Fisher-A18.pdf`) | Flammables cabinet |
> | 91% IPA | Highly flammable liquid, H225 (`SDS-Isopropyl-Alcohol-91-Alliance.pdf`) | Flammables cabinet |
> | LiME LiNE clear coat, Part A | Flam. Liq. 2, flash point below −18 °C (`LIMELINE-SDS-UC4-1-Clearcoat-Part-A.pdf`) | Flammables cabinet |
> | LiME LiNE clear coat, Part B (activator) | SDS not on file; an isocyanate | Flammables cabinet, not in a tray with an amine hardener |
> | IN2, AT30 SLOW and FAST, XCR, West 105, 205, 206, 209 | Not flammable liquids: flash points from above 93 °C to 150 °C, none with a flammable class (SDS §9) | Flammables cabinet, because campus EH&S filed them there. They are not there for being flammable |
>
> [co-storage paragraph from §1, if accepted]

Process-rule weight: the storage locations do not change, so this is a
correction ("wrong" in the register), but the co-storage paragraph that goes
with it is a rule change.

### Tradeoffs

- Writing "not flammable" next to the epoxies could read as permission to
  store them anywhere. The Store in column answers that: they stay in the
  cabinet.
- Keeping five non-flammable systems in a rated cabinet uses space the real
  flammables need. If the cabinet fills, the epoxies are the ones that can
  move out, to any dry, cool shelf away from heat and sunlight (every epoxy
  SDS §7). Nothing in the SDSs requires a rated cabinet for them.
- Adding IPA to the list is new, but the old list was the team's shorthand
  for "the solvents", and IPA is one.

## 3. Related: expired material (R-014-07)

Not CS-011 §6, but the audit raised it against CS-011 (G-09) and CS-014 §7.9
now marks the spot.

**Current text:** none. CS-001 §7.6 puts the expiry on the label; nothing
says what happens when it passes.

**What the app does:** a lot past its Expires date shows "N expired" on its
shelf (`app/inventory.js` lotExpired). The State list has Expired
(`app/shop.js` LOT_STATE). The cure buy-off does not check expiry, so
nothing stops an expired jug going on a part. R-008-11 (decided) adds a
"should": check the expiry before mixing and ask a lead if it is past.

**What the TDSs say:** IN2 and XCR have a 12-month shelf life stored as the
TDS says; "it may be possible to use the resin after a longer period" but
"a deterioration in the performance of the resin will occur, especially in
relation to clarity and cure profile" (`03 Datasheets/EC-TDS-IN2-Infusion-Resin.pdf`,
`03 Datasheets/EC-TDS-XCR-Epoxy-Coating-Resin.pdf`).

**Candidate wording for CS-014 §7.9 (and a pointer from CS-011):**

> Do not use an expired resin, hardener or XCR on a season part. A lead may
> release it for R&D or a test panel after a small test cure, recorded on the
> lot. Set its State to Expired, and dispose of it through campus EH&S.

**Tradeoffs:** the TDS supports a test cure before use (clarity and cure
profile are what degrade). The cost is a test panel's worth of resin and a
lead's time. The open part is disposal: no RFS route is in the code or Slack.
Nick (open question 7) needs to supply it before this lands.
